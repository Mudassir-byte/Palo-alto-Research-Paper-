Abstract

We perform an end-to-end diagnostic evaluation of the supplied human resources dataset (all 21 automated validation checks passed; zero missing values, zero duplicates, and zero out-of-range ratings across 1,470 records and 31 fields). We formalize a four-item Composite Engagement Index (CEI) on a 25–100 scale and a rule-based burnout screening proxy, evaluating their distribution across workload factors, job roles, organizational hierarchies, and career tenure stages.

The population mean CEI is 68.1 (95% CI 67.4 to 68.7). Under the deterministic screening rule (OverTime = Yes and WorkLifeBalance ≤ 2), 8.6% (126 records) are classified as High screening level, 40.0% (588 records) as Medium, and 51.4% (756 records) as Low. Engagement scores demonstrate remarkable statistical uniformity across departments, roles, job levels, travel intensity, and commute bands (all omnibus ANOVA and Kruskal–Wallis tests yield p > 0.40).

Crucially, two empirical findings contradict intuitive clinical assumptions:

  • The Overtime Engagement Paradox: Records working overtime report a statistically higher mean CEI than non-overtime records (69.6 vs 67.5, difference +2.13, Welch p = 0.003, d = 0.17).
  • Orthogonality of Satisfaction Facets: The four survey items that compose the index are essentially uncorrelated (mean inter-item correlation ρ = -0.0012; Cronbach’s α = -0.0071), proving that CEI acts as an index of heterogeneous facets rather than a unidimensional psychometric construct.

The single strongest empirical association is observed with historical Attrition: records flagged as having departed exhibit a CEI 6.79 points lower than retained employees (62.4 vs 69.2, d = -0.58, p < 0.001), and the departed share is 35.7% in the High burnout screening cohort versus 12.4% in the Low cohort. These patterns represent cross-sectional associations. We outline strict privacy-preserving safeguards (minimum cohort size k ≥ 5) and recommend aggregate workload interventions rather than individual surveillance.

1. Problem Context and Objectives

Modern enterprise human resources operations suffer from a chronic structural limitation: strategic interventions are typically reactive and late-stage. Workforce attrition and severe disengagement are predominantly evaluated retrospectively via exit interviews or quarterly retrospective reports. Organizations routinely lack a formalized, unified engagement metric, clear diagnostic visibility into burnout-vulnerable employee cohorts, and empirical understanding of how structural demands (e.g., compulsory overtime, extensive travel, long commutes) interact with psychological resources (e.g., job involvement, peer relationships, supervisor support).

This project frames workforce strain through the lens of the established Job Demands–Resources (JD–R) model:

📚 Theoretical Foundation: Job Demands–Resources (JD–R) Paradigm

Employee wellbeing is modeled as a dynamic equilibrium between Job Demands (physical, psychological, and organizational efforts such as overtime hours, business travel frequency, and commute strain) and Job Resources (factors that reduce physiological costs, achieve work goals, and stimulate personal growth, including organizational involvement, role satisfaction, and relational support).

Using exclusively the supplied workforce records, this diagnostic study addresses four empirical questions:

  • RQ1 (Aggregate Distribution): Where are engagement deficits structurally located across organizational strata?
  • RQ2 (Workload Intersectionality): In which departments and job roles do mandatory overtime and depressed work-life balance co-occur?
  • RQ3 (Career Stagnation): Do role tenure and promotion latency correlate with measurable reductions in engagement?
  • RQ4 (Attrition Lineage): How do engagement and burnout proxies correlate descriptively with employee attrition flags?

3. Methodology and Metrics Formalization

3.1 Mathematical Definitions

The analysis formalizes two source-mandated indicators and three proposed heuristic metrics to provide diagnostic transparency:

Metric 1: Composite Engagement Index (CEI) — Source Mandated
$$\text{CEI} = \frac{\text{JobInvolvement} + \text{JobSatisfaction} + \text{EnvironmentSatisfaction} + \text{RelationshipSatisfaction}}{16} \times 100$$
Range: [25.0, 100.0] · Scaled linearly from the sum of four 1–4 Likert items · Minimum observed 31.25, Maximum 100.0 · Strict policy: any missing facet prevents CEI calculation (no imputation).
Metric 2: Rule-Based Burnout Screening Proxy — Source Mandated
$$\text{Screening Level} = \begin{cases} \textbf{High}, & \text{if } \text{OverTime} = \text{'Yes'} \land \text{WorkLifeBalance} \le 2 \\ \textbf{Medium}, & \text{if } (\text{OverTime} = \text{'Yes'} \oplus \text{WorkLifeBalance} \le 2) \\ \textbf{Low}, & \text{if } \text{OverTime} = \text{'No'} \land \text{WorkLifeBalance} > 2 \end{cases}$$
Classification proxy only · Designed to flag aggregate conditions where demands outpace recovery · Explicitly NOT a clinical diagnosis or individualized psychiatric tool.
Metrics 3 & 4: Workload Stress (WSI) & Career Stagnation (CSI) — Proposed Heuristics
$$\text{WSI} = 100 \times \left[ \frac{1}{3} \cdot \omega_{\text{travel}} + \frac{1}{3} \cdot \left(\frac{\text{Distance} - 1}{28}\right) + \frac{1}{3} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\text{OverTime}} \right]$$ $$\text{CSI} = 100 \times \left[ 0.5 \cdot \min\left(1, \frac{\text{YearsSinceLastPromotion}}{\text{YearsAtCompany}}\right) + 0.5 \cdot \min\left(1, \frac{\text{YearsInCurrentRole}}{\text{YearsAtCompany}}\right) \right]$$
WSI weights: travel (None=0, Rarely=0.5, Frequently=1.0), Commute clipped to [0,1], Overtime (0 or 1). Equal weights. · CSI defined strictly for employees with \(\text{YearsAtCompany} \ge 2\).

3.2 Privacy Preserving & Differential Safeguards

To ensure ethical deployment in accordance with modern privacy standards, the analytical engine enforces three strict structural safeguards:

  • Minimum Cohort Threshold (\(k \ge 5\)): Any reporting subgroup with fewer than 5 records is automatically suppressed or combined into an aggregate "Other" category.
  • Secondary Suppression: When only a single cell in a marginal distribution falls below 5, the second smallest cell is also withheld to prevent algebraic reconstruction by subtraction.
  • Demographic Blindness in Operational Prompts: Gender and marital status are strictly excluded from automated risk algorithms or managerial action prompts. They are analyzed exclusively within an optional aggregated audit for demographic parity.

4. Empirical Findings

4.1 Composite Engagement Index Distribution

The distribution of CEI across all 1,470 records exhibits a parametric mean of 68.08 (SD = 12.60, median = 68.75, IQR = 62.5–75.0, range = 31.25–100.0). Only 69 records (4.69%) fall below the critical threshold of 50 (which corresponds to an average Likert item score below 2 out of 4).

⚠ Psychometric Warning: Near-Zero Cronbach's Alpha (α = -0.007)

The mean inter-item Pearson correlation among the four survey components is ρ = -0.0012, yielding a standardized Cronbach's alpha of α = -0.0071. In this dataset, the four survey questions behave as statistically independent, orthogonal measurements. A low composite score does not imply pervasive disengagement across all domains, and CEI should be treated as a composite checklist rather than a unified latent psychological trait.

4.2 Burnout Screening Distribution

Under the deterministic screening rule, 8.57% of records (n = 126) meet the High criteria, 40.00% (n = 588) meet Medium criteria, and 51.43% (n = 756) are Low risk. Exactly 28.30% of records log overtime, and 28.84% report low work-life balance (≤ 2). Across departments, the High share is 8.85% in R&D (85 records), 8.74% in Sales (39 records), and 3.17% in HR (2 records; cell size < 5 applies). No organizational division approaches the 15% threshold specified in the strategic plan for mandatory workload review.

4.3 The Overtime Paradox & Workload Conditions

Conventional organizational theories predict that compulsory overtime depresses employee morale. Across the 9 job roles, mean engagement spans an exceedingly narrow band: from 66.64 (Research Directors) to 69.12 (Managers) — a total spread of merely 2.48 points. 4.5 Career Tenure & Stagnation Dynamics

Empirical evaluation reveals zero meaningful correlation between engagement and organizational tenure (ρ = 0.004), role tenure (ρ = -0.024), years since last promotion (ρ = -0.019), or the Career Stagnation Index (ρ = 0.007, n = 1,255). The assumption that stagnation systematically depresses survey engagement is not supported by these data.

5. Discussion & Theoretical Interpretation

Synthesized across all dimensions, the data depict a workforce characterized by high cross-sectional engagement uniformity across roles, job levels, and organizational divisions. The prominent finding that overtime records report slightly higher engagement (+2.13 CEI points) while simultaneously exhibiting triple the historical attrition rate (30.5% vs 10.4%) reveals a crucial psychological nuance:

Employees who work overtime in this organization may represent highly involved, conscientious personnel who derive genuine immediate satisfaction from their work duties (elevating short-term survey scores). However, persistent structural workload demands without proportional physiological recovery eventually deplete cognitive reserves, accelerating eventual turnover.

6. Epistemic Limitations

  • Cross-Sectional Architecture: The dataset is a single static snapshot. Longitudinal persistence over quarters cannot be verified, preventing early-warning alerting.
  • Absence of Exit Timestamps: Attrition is recorded as a binary flag without exit dates, making it impossible to determine whether survey ratings preceded or followed resignation decisions.
  • Zero Diagnostic Predictive Claims: No predictive machine learning model is trained or validated on this data; benchmark ROI or turnover-reduction percentages cannot be established.
  • Psychometric Heterogeneity: Cronbach's α = -0.007 indicates CEI cannot serve as an automated clinical alert without redesigned survey instruments.

7. Ethical Safeguards & Responsible HR AI

All algorithmic metrics are framed explicitly as prompts to investigate aggregate work conditions, never as performance evaluations or individual disciplinary triggers. Individual rows, employee-level identifiers, and cross-filtered cells under k = 5 are strictly suppressed. Leadership training is mandated prior to dashboard access to eliminate managerial misinterpretation risks.

8. Actionable Recommendations

✓ Near-Term Low-Regret Actions
  • Conduct team-level workload reviews where overtime is concentrated (>30%).
  • Initiate career progression pathway dialogues for Sales Representatives.
  • Cease any ranking or punitive comparisons of roles based on CEI (differences are noise).
